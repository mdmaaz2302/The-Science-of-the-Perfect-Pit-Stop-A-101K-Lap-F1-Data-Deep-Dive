Methodology notes

  • Started from f1_strategy_dataset_v6.csv (101,371 rows). Five cleaning decisions applied, each investigated before acting: dropped 66 rows of missing Compound (two full-stint telemetry gaps: RUS/Austria 2022, STR/Monaco 2023), dropped 1 row of undefined PitNextLap (the dataset's final recorded lap), filled 1,848 missing Prev_TyreLife with 0 (structurally undefined on a driver's first lap, not unknown), flagged (not dropped) 1 row of lap-1 laps per session as Is_Racing_Lap = 0 since lap 1 includes grid/formation-lap effects that distort pace, and dropped 5,062 pre-season session rows since Position isn't meaningful outside competitive sessions.
  • Final clean dataset: 96,242 rows, zero missing values, exported as f1_clean.csv and loaded into SQLite (f1.db, table laps) for the four strategy queries.
  • Model: PitNextLap predicted from TyreLife, Cumulative_Degradation, Compound, Position, LapTime_Delta, and Stint. Only ~2.9% of laps are followed by a pit stop, so both models use class_weight='balanced' and are compared on ROC-AUC rather than raw accuracy.
  • Random Forest (300 trees, max_depth=10) clearly outperforms Logistic Regression on ROC-AUC (0.889 vs. 0.784) — pit decisions hinge on non-linear combinations of tyre age, degradation, and pace that tree splits capture better than a linear boundary.
Small-sample flag: the "Late" pit-timing bucket (first stop after 66% race distance) has only n=105 driver-races, mostly incident- or retirement-affected races where scoring around a DNF gets erratic. Its median (0) is more trustworthy than its mean (−30.5, an outlier-driven artifact), but read it with caution either way.
